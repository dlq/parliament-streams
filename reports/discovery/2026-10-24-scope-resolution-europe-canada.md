## Assemblée réunie de la Commission communautaire commune / Verenigde Vergadering van de Gemeenschappelijke Gemeenschapscommissie

Original candidate: `scope-review--belgium--assemblee-reunie-de-la-commission-communautaire-commune-verenigde-vergadering-van-de-gemeenschappelijke-gemeenschapscommissie`.

**eligible_legislature** — Retain as a distinct eligible statutory legislative formation sharing the Brussels Parliament membership; create a formation relationship rather than an extra territory or separately elected chamber.

Special Law of 12 January 1989 article 68(1) expressly gives the Assemblée réunie and Collège réuni joint ordinance-making authority and gives the members initiative. The Chamber factsheet independently identifies bicommunity institutions and personal matters including health and social policy. This is substantive lawmaking over assigned community competences.

Article 82(1) applies the Special Financing Law article 50 annual budget, accounts and Court of Audit framework to COCOM with adaptations. The April 2026 official committee agenda addresses oral questions and requests for explanations to Collège réuni ministers; scrutiny is exercised in this legislative formation.

## Nunatsiavut Assembly

Original candidate: `scope-review--canada--nunatsiavut-assembly`.

**eligible_legislature** — Add the Nunatsiavut Assembly as a regional autonomous special-status legislature under the Labrador Inuit treaty/constitution. Provincial nesting does not remove its regional legislative status.

The treaty establishes legislative and executive institutions and grants lawmaking in substantive fields, including education and health (chapter 17, parts 17.12 and 17.13). Labrador Inuit Constitution 4.18.1 vests that treaty-defined lawmaking competence in the Assembly; 4.18.2 requires Assembly passage and presidential assent. This establishes a regional legislature, beyond collective ownership or consultation.

Constitution 4.1.3 assigns lawmaking and government oversight. Sections 3.2.7–3.2.8 require the First Minister to retain Assembly confidence; 8.8.3 requires Assembly approval of the annual consolidated financial plan. Assembly Act 51–53 provides witness/document powers with jurisdictional and judicial/independent-body exceptions.

- [Labrador Inuit Land Claims Agreement, official federal publication](https://www.rcaanc-cirnac.gc.ca/eng/1293647179208/1542904949105): 2.1.1; 17.2.1; 17.3.3; 17.12.1–17.12.6; 17.13.1. Treaty-protected self-government requires legislative/executive institutions and provides actual education/health lawmaking; competence and conflict rules are specifically delimited. Evidence: `primary_agreement_text_review`. Official federal treaty text read on 24 October 2026. This finding concerns the established legislative framework, not a legal opinion on every later amendment.
- [Labrador Inuit Constitution, Schedule A to Nunatsiavut Constitution Act](https://www.nunatsiavut.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/IL-2005-02-Nunatsiavut-Constitution-Act_.pdf): 3.2.7–3.2.8; 4.1.1–4.1.3; 4.18.1–4.18.8; 8.8.3. Assembly is the regional parliament, enacts treaty-competent Inuit laws, approves the government financial plan and sustains the First Minister through confidence. Evidence: `primary_pdf_section_text_review`. Original enacted 1 December 2005 source; core authority corroborated by treaty and maintained current legislation index. Not a fresh consolidated membership/seat-count certification.
- [Nunatsiavut Assembly Act, CIL N-2 (30-11-2021)](https://www.nunatsiavut.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/CIL-N-2-30-11-2021-Nunatsiavut-Assembly-Act.pdf): sections 51–53; parts 2 and 7. Act provides the Assembly/committees with powers to compel witnesses and documents, subject to stated jurisdictional and judicial/independent-body limits; legislative-process part is identified. Evidence: `primary_search_index_statute_section_text_review`. Consolidation through 30 November 2021 lists amendments through IL 2021-04. PDF reader reported 22 pages but no text and screenshots cache-missed; direct PDF retrieval returned403. Actual indexed sections 51–53 were read; no claim of full-PDF inspection.
- [Nunatsiavut Government maintained legislation register](https://nunatsiavut.com/legislation/): IL 2026-02 Budget Act 2026; IL 2026-03 Inuit Communities Quarries Act 2026; constitutional and Assembly Acts. Maintained government register identifies 2026 budget and substantive legislation, alongside the regional constitutional/Assembly framework. Evidence: `primary_page_content_review`. Register reviewed 24 October 2026; 2026 Budget item dated 6 March and Quarries Act item dated 24 September. These are continuity/identity pointers, not a review of programme guides or individual websites.

Scope/currentness limits:

- This is not a fourth Canadian territory or a province. Retain the autonomous Inuit government relationship within Newfoundland and Labrador.
- Treaty lawmaking has subject, territorial and personal limits; federal/provincial conflict rules differ by field. Do not describe it as unlimited sovereignty.
- Local Inuit Community Councils remain a separate local-government class; the regional Assembly decision does not automatically include them.
- The legal decision does not certify current membership, every constitutional amendment, or media availability.
## Sámi Parliament of Finland

Original candidate: `scope-review--finland--sami-parliament-of-finland`.

**outside_default_scope** — Retain in the excluded/boundary ledger as an Indigenous cultural self-government and representative body. Its current enacted powers do not establish an autonomous territorial legislature under the project rule.

Act 974/1995 as amended by Act 414/2025 sections 1,5–6 establishes cultural/linguistic self-government, own decisions in that remit, representation, initiatives and proposals. Sections9–9 b strengthen negotiation/cooperation aimed at agreement or consent for state measures affecting Sámi rights. They do not confer a general power to enact substantive territorial laws.

Sections2,8 and13–15 provide state financing, autonomous use of operating funds and cultural grants, and an internal board implementing plenary decisions. These are real self-government powers, but managing institutional grants/operations and participating in state decisions does not satisfy the territorial lawmaking and territorial executive-control gate.

Enacted powers concern self-government in language/culture, representation, own grants/operations and strengthened public-authority cooperation/negotiation. No territorial legislative grant appears in those operative provisions. Evidence: `primary_pdf_statute_section_text_review`. Promulgated27 June 2025; published2 July 2025; in force1 August 2025. Finnish operative provisions and commencement read; older English translations were not substituted.

## Holy Community of Mount Athos

Original candidate: `scope-review--greece--holy-community-of-mount-athos`.

**outside_default_scope** — Resolve the original candidate as the wrong organ for the legislative inventory: Holy Community is the regular administrative body. Retain a cross-reference to the distinct extraordinary twenty-member Synaxis as the qualifying autonomous legislative organ.

Constitution 105(2) assigns administration to the Holy Community. Charter 43 identifies the extraordinary twenty-member Synaxis as the highest legislative/judicial organ and expressly excludes the regular representatives from that legislative body. The original Community name must not absorb the Synaxis lawmaking powers.

Charter 35 makes the Community approve the common-fund budget and additional expenses and inspect executive accounts; Holy Epistasia executes. Administrative finance control alone does not make this particular organ the legislature.

- [Constitution of Greece](https://www.hellenicparliament.gr/Vouli-ton-Ellinon/To-Politevma/Syntagma/article-109/): article105(1)–(5). Self-government/Greek sovereignty, Holy Community administration, Charter ratification and state/Patriarchal supervision are explicit. Evidence: `primary_constitution_full_article_review`. Current Parliament constitutional article read24 October 2026; English parliamentary PDF also checked.
- [Charter of Mount Athos10 May 1924 and ratifying legislative decree 10/16 September 1926](https://www.esphigmenou.gr/p/kxao.html): Charter 11,14,35–36,40,43; decree 6. Own monastery law text separates administrative Community, executive Epistasia and legislative Synaxis. Synaxis fixes compulsory fund contributions; non-spiritual regulations require ministerial confirmation. Evidence: `primary_constituent_institution_statute_text_review`. Undated institutional republication of foundational law; not certified as an exhaustive 2026 consolidation.
- [Greek Army government guide](https://stelexi.army.gr/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/odigos_1i_tax_kd-al_okt_21.pdf): Mount Athos governance description. Independently describes Synaxis legislative, Holy Community administrative and Epistasia executive roles. Evidence: `primary_search_index_government_guide_excerpt_review`. Guide labelled October 2021 and hosted 2024. Corroborating institutional description; not sole source of legislative powers.

Scope/currentness limits:

- Mount Athos remains a constitutionally self-governed part of Greece; Greek sovereignty and state public-order supervision are preserved.
- Do not classify every monastery council, spiritual tribunal or the Holy Epistasia as a legislature.
- The identity correction does not exclude all Mount Athos territorial lawmaking; the corrected Synaxis is recorded separately below.
- The Charter/decree text is published by a constituent monastery. It is not a current consolidated state-gazette certificate; current constitutional article105 independently confirms the governing framework.

### Corrected Athos legislative identity

**Extraordinary Twenty-Member Synaxis of Mount Athos / Έκτακτη Εικοσαμελής Σύναξη — eligible.** Charter 43 names the legislative organ; ratifying decree 6 empowers it to enact regulations consistent with the Charter, subject to ministerial confirmation (Patriarchal approval for purely spiritual provisions). Charter 36 grants determination of compulsory common-fund contributions; decree 33 provides for setting court expenses/fees by Synaxis regulation.

The constitutional territorial regime and binding non-spiritual rules/compulsory territorial institutional revenue clear the autonomous special-status bar. Spiritual/monastic status alone neither establishes nor defeats eligibility. Finance/executive administration is divided among the Synaxis, Community and Epistasia; record their distinct roles.

Foundational law was read from a constituent institution; no claim that every 1926 provision remains wholly unamended. Current Constitution 105 and independent government description corroborate organ separation.

### Council of the Cree Nation Government / Conseil du Gouvernement de la nation crie

Suggested inventory ID: `canada--cree-nation-eeyou-istchee`. **Eligible regional autonomous legislature.** Add the regional Cree Nation Government council in its treaty lawmaking capacity. Keep the Grand Council corporate/representative identity and the mixed Eeyou Istchee James Bay Regional Government separate.

Governance Agreement 2.1–2.3 establishes local/regional arrangements;4.8 defines regional territorial reach;8.12 gives environmental/natural-resource/pollution lawmaking and8.15 direct taxation. Constitution 11.1–11.3 places enactment/publication in the regional council. These operative regional treaty powers establish more than a coordinating service board.

Quebec Act respecting the Cree Nation Government(G-1.031)46–49 assigns executive powers through council bylaws/appointments;81–83 requires annual council budget adoption and accessible accounts. Constitution 11 establishes a public regional lawmaking procedure.

Signed18 July 2017; framework reviewed24 October 2026. No inference that every conditional jurisdiction is currently exercised.
