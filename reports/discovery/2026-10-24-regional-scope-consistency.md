The same test applies throughout: first-level or qualifying combined territorial status; substantive own-remit regional norms; and representative budget/executive control. Neither the word “council” nor a national legal text’s broad “local government” heading settles eligibility. Municipal councils and a narrow service/coordination mandate remain excluded. This is a project scope classification, not a claim that every included body is legally described as a state parliament.

## Czech regions and Croatia

[Czech Constitution Articles 99–105](https://www.psp.cz/docs/laws/constitution.html) distinguish higher territorial self-government and generally binding ordinances from delegated administration. The [ministry Regions Act translation](https://mv.gov.cz/soubor/act-no-129-2000-coll-on-regions-establishment-of-regions.aspx), sections 2, 4, 6–8, confirms that distinction and legal publication. Its section 6 also requires statutory support for imposing duties: the existing inclusion never meant unlimited regional lawmaking. The translation incorporates amendments through 2002, not every current procedural amendment.

[Croatia’s Constitution Articles 133–138](https://www.sabor.hr/en/information-access/important-legislation/constitution-republic-croatia-consolidated-text) gives counties a separate regional self-government tier, broad regional affairs, autonomy and own revenues. The [official consolidated LRSG Act](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2013_02_19_323.html), Articles 35–36, 71 and 73–76, supplies own-remit general acts, budget control, executive questioning/reports, publication and implementation affecting rights/obligations. The [responsible ministry’s current explanation](https://mpudt.gov.hr/sustav-lokalne-i-podrucne-regionalne-samouprave-23640/23640), especially questions 54–56, corroborates the core functions after that historical consolidation.

These powers satisfy the accepted Czech functional rationale. “Service administration only” understates the Croatian statute. Zagreb is assessed in its full combined county capacity, supported by its [own statute, Articles 39–41](https://skupstina.zagreb.hr/UserDocsImages/Engleski%20WEB/Statut%20Grada%20Zagreba_engleski%20prijevod.pdf). Other Croatian cities do not qualify merely because they perform a county service. Historical seat counts, currencies and executive appointment mechanics were not adopted.

## Hungary: distinguish Budapest from counties

The [official Fundamental Law download](https://njt.jog.gov.hu/jogszabaly/en/2011-4301-02-00) is a Ministry of Justice PDF **as in force on 1 October 2026**. Articles 32–33 establish own-remit decrees, autonomous administration, budgets and representative authority. This ordinary public download was read directly after the web reader timed out.

The [current native Local Government Act](https://njt.jog.gov.hu/jogszabaly/2011-189-00-00), also dated 1 October 2026, explicitly gives Budapest combined territorial/municipal capacity in sections 3(3) and 22. Sections 23–26 cover capital-wide and cross-district regulation; section 42 reserves decree-making to the assembly. The capital therefore meets the same combined territorial-capital rationale used for Prague and Zagreb. Ordinary district councils and county-right cities do not gain eligibility from that decision.

Keep the 19 counties outside scope because section 27’s operative county remit is development, rural development and coordination. General decree and voluntary-task powers were considered; the reviewed grant does not establish broad normative authority beyond that county-development remit. The current statute uses **megye** again; its dated terminology differs from the older fixed June 2024 text. Individual institution-name updates belong in the later metadata pass.

## Other exclusions: retain real powers and their limits

**Albania:** [Law 139/2015 Article 31](https://qarkuvlore.gov.al/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/Ligj-Nr.1392015-Per-veteqeverisjen-vendore.pdf) gives qark policy coordination/harmonisation and allocated/delegated functions; Articles 72–77 and 85 place the council within that remit. [Elbasan’s own functions page](https://qarkuelbasan.gov.al/funksionet-e-qarkut/) corroborates the framework through indexed text. The exclusion rests on function, not indirect election or absence of binding decisions. This is not a complete 2026 statutory consolidation.

**Guyana:** constitutional autonomy and binding decisions are acknowledged. The [Ministry of Legal Affairs’ actual chapter download](https://www.mola.gov.gy/public/laws/Volume%208%20Cap.%2028.04%20-%2036.011696911949.pdf), Cap. 28:09 section 7, requires ministerial approval for local-area regulations; sections 13, 32–33 apply the RDC/local-administration framework and national-policy alignment. Together those limits support the exclusion. The downloaded chapter is a 2012 reprint; newer hosting does not certify a 2026 consolidation. Future contrary enacted grants can reopen the finding.

**Mongolia:** constitutional local resolutions, budgets and governor scrutiny are real. The [2020 revised Units and Governance Act](https://legalinfo.mn/en/edtl/16532201415661), especially Article 35.1.14, conditions administrative normative acts on special statutory empowerment and registration. Assigned local functions and sectoral trade/service procedures differ from a general autonomous regional legislative mandate. The state portal labels its English text an unofficial translation; every sectoral delegation was not individually reviewed.
