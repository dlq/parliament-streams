## Indonesia

DPD’s constitutional initiative and discussion of regional bills are substantive national legislative participation, alongside budget advice and scrutiny. Final ordinary-bill agreement remains with DPR and the President. The Constitutional Court makes that distinction expressly in reasons [3.18.1]–[3.18.3], printed pp. 244–248 of [decision 92/PUU-X/2012](https://mkri.id/public/content/persidangan/putusan/putusan_sidang_92%20PUU%202012%20-%20DPD%20-%20telah%20ucap%2027%20Maret%202013.pdf). [DPD’s own explanation](https://orchard.dpd.go.id/profile/fungsi-tugas-wewenang) confirms its legislative, budget and scrutiny remit. Preserve the IPU directory classification separately from this broader constitutional-body inventory.

MPR is eligible as the constitutional amendment/adoption organ, with DPR and DPD members and specified presidential constitutional functions. Its membership overlaps those bodies; adding it creates no additional national jurisdiction or independent ordinary chamber. [MPR’s own institutional explanation](https://www.mpr.go.id/tentang-mpr/Kedudukan%2C-Tugas%2C-dan-Wewenang) supplies Constitution Articles 2–3, 7B, 8, 37 and Law 17/2014 as amended. The own page was available through indexed text; direct access returned 403. Current individual membership was not reviewed.

## Tokelau

The three Taupulega have real statutory village-rulemaking powers. The [Village Incorporation Regulations 1986](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/secondary-legislation/pco-drafted/1991/4/en/2007-09-03/DLM117935/) establish corporate villages, management, finances and binding rules for village/island affairs, including taxes and offences. The rules remain subordinate to General Fono rules. [Tokelau Act 1948 section 3A](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1948/24/en/latest/#dlm247577) separately grants General Fono broad territorial legislative power.

Their confirmed remit supports an **ordinary village/local-government exclusion**, despite their traditional importance and remoteness. [Government’s administration account](https://www.tokelau.org.nz/About+Us/Administration.html), describing Taupulega as the highest source of delegated authority, is retained and weighed. That summary does not by itself establish three separate autonomous territorial legislatures. The [own-government 2008 history](https://www.tokelau.org.nz/site/tokelau/files/Tokelau%20A%20History%20of%20Government%20FINAL.pdf) distinguishes 2004 executive delegation from 1996 statutory national lawmaking. Eligibility was decided from proved functions and the chosen scope, not from the word “council” or geographic nesting alone.

Do not substitute the referendum draft for an operative constitution. The [Self Determination Package](https://www.tokelau.org.nz/About+Us/Government/Self+Determination+Package.html) identifies the draft; the [MFAT-origin PDF’s schedule 1](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/tok209463.pdf) conditions commencement on repeal of the 1948 Act, still listed in force. Its proposed village veto cannot alone prove present authority.

These exclusions can be reopened on concrete contrary evidence from:

1. The operative internal Constitution of Tokelau, its adoption/commencement instrument and amendments.
2. The signed 2004 Administrator delegation and Taupulega national-function subdelegation, with replacements/amendments.
3. The operative Village Incorporation Rules and subsequent General Fono amendments establishing autonomous territorial legislative status beyond the proved village remit.

The NZ village-law reprint stops at 1997; the 2016 Tokelau consolidation returned 410. These currentness limits are explicit reopening leads, rather than an invented absence of village legislative authority or a generic authority hold.

The [2014 government report, printed p. 16](https://www.tokelau.org.nz/site/tokelau/TNSP%20Results%20Matrix%20as%20at%2030%20June%202014.pdf) lists constitutional amendments under review; July 2013 endorsement concerns the Constitution Committee review. The [released 2022 General Fono decisions, printed pp. 4–6](https://www.mfat.govt.nz/assets/OIA/OIA-2023/OIA-28717-Tokelau-Briefings-27-July-2023.pdf) mention 2013 election-rule amendments. Neither establishes a New Zealand “Tokelau Amendment Act 2013”.
