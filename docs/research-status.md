## Current Local State — 2026-10-22

These counts describe the working tree, including the latest five territorial
additions; they do not assert that uncommitted work is already deployed.
`data/channels.json` remains the canonical published-data source.

- 117 sources: 50 national, 61 subnational/territorial, six supranational.
- 42 distinct published regional/territorial jurisdictions, all with map geometry.
- 38 native-player sources and three provider embeds; 63 official link-outs and
  13 research-only records.
- 33 catalogue records have implemented schedule metadata, covering 17 unique
  source URLs. Another 89 unique schedule/event URLs remain planned.
- 50 records have a pending rights status. All 117 retain at least one unknown
  media-accessibility field; unknown does not mean unavailable.
- 23 tracked candidate records: 19 promoted and four researching. The latter
  are Chile Sala IV, Iceland Althingi, Skåne and France's Senate. Other discovery
  leads remain in research reports before becoming formal candidates.

## Wider Country And Territory Audit

The national ledger records an initial official-source discovery pass for all
71 maintained Tier 1/Tier 2 countries. The separate regional ledger contains
1,121 initial searches in 58 country audit groups, plus 13 scope dispositions
where reviewed evidence did not establish a core regional legislature.
Special-status completeness, legal-authority assessment and service validation
remain open. Discovery counts are not eligible-parliament or stream counts.

The agreed scope is substantive legislative authority. Ordinary councils and
planning/service bodies are outside default catalogue scope; borderline
regional assemblies need institution-specific authority evidence. Country audit
groupings are navigation associations, not sovereignty assertions.

The first eight official-link additions were Japan's two chamber services,
Austria, Mauritius, Italy's lower house, Nagasaki committees, Córdoba's
provincial Legislature and Santa Catarina TV Alesc. Five more clear
parliamentary services followed: Jersey States Assembly, Tynwald (audio and
committee video), French Polynesia Assembly, New Caledonia Congress and
Sint Maarten Parliament. These thirteen additions did not add direct HLS,
provider embeds or implemented XMLTV parsers.

The August 14 national refresh added Germany's Bundestag channel 1 as a validated but
permission-pending national HLS source, reconfirmed several existing national
sources, and downgraded endpoints that did not validate cleanly.

The supranational pass added official-page entries for European Parliament
Multimedia Centre, Council of the European Union Live, EU Audiovisual Service /
EBS, United Nations Web TV, Council of Europe / PACE Live, and OSCE Live. These
are link-out/schedule targets; no stable raw HLS/DASH manifest was validated
for them in the static or browser pass.

The non-US sub-national work records Nunavut and Jalisco as validated,
permission-pending direct HLS sources. Scottish Parliament TV, Senedd TV, and
Northern Ireland Assembly TV are documented as official-page and schedule
entries with source-specific rights guidance. The 2026-08-16 promotion pass
added 18 more official-player and schedule records covering Canadian
provinces and territories, Australian states, German Landtage, and Spanish
autonomous parliaments. These remain link-out sources unless a stable direct
feed and suitable playback posture are both documented. Colima remains
excluded because the validated regional broadcaster has not been established
as a parliamentary channel.
