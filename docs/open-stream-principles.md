# Open Stream Principles

Parliament Streams advocates for public legislative video to be open in more
than one sense. A publicly viewable page alone is not enough for durable,
equitable public access.

## What We Mean By Open

- **Open delivery:** publish streams through documented, interoperable web
  protocols and direct URLs, rather than making a proprietary player the only
  route to proceedings. HTTP Live Streaming is documented in
  [RFC 8216](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8216).
- **Open access:** make live and archived proceedings reachable without an
  account, a special app, artificial geographic restrictions, or unnecessary
  technical barriers, except where a lawful restriction is genuinely required.
- **Open use:** publish clear terms that allow public viewing, embedding,
  quotation, preservation, reporting, and other appropriate reuse. The
  [Open Definition](https://opendefinition.org/) provides a useful benchmark:
  open material can be accessed, used, modified, and shared by anyone, subject
  at most to conditions that preserve provenance and openness.
- **Open discovery:** provide stable watch pages, machine-readable metadata,
  direct endpoint information where appropriate, programme schedules, and
  historical records so that proceedings can be found and independently
  verified.
- **Open accessibility:** publish captions, transcripts, sign-language
  interpretation where available, and accessible players and metadata. The
  W3C's [guidance on accessible audio and video](https://www.w3.org/WAI/media/av/)
  outlines the relevant practices.

## The Project's Role

This catalogue documents the state of access and recorded provider terms; it
does not imply that every listed source satisfies these principles or that
technical access itself grants a licence. The catalogue exists in part to make
gaps visible and to support public institutions, journalists, civic groups,
and stream providers who want parliamentary video to be easier to find, use,
preserve, and scrutinize.
