# What belongs in Parliament Streams

The catalogue is for legislatures with **legislative authority**: national
parliaments and their chambers, and state, provincial, territorial, autonomous
or devolved legislatures. Public committee proceedings of an eligible
legislature belong in scope too.

This working rule reflects the project decision of 22 October 2026. The territorial
boundary was confirmed on 24 October: **first-level territorial legislatures and
autonomous special-status legislatures**. An autonomous regional institution can
qualify while nested within a state or province. This pass does not enumerate every
municipal, village, Indigenous or tribal government below that tier.

The wider discovery inventory deliberately searched more broadly; inclusion in that
research ledger is not a decision to publish a body in the catalogue.

## Research priorities and democracy tiers

We use the **Economist Intelligence Unit (EIU) Democracy Index** to help order
country-by-country research. It assesses elections and pluralism, government,
political participation, political culture and civil liberties. These are EIU's
classifications, not ratings produced by Parliament Streams.
[EIU's methodology and report hub](https://www.eiu.com/n/global-themes/democracy-index/).

The maintained discovery targets currently retain the **2024 index baseline,
published in February 2025**:

- **Tier 1:** 25 countries classified as full democracies.
- **Tier 2:** 46 classified as flawed democracies.
- Together these define the existing **71-country research sweep**, extended to
  eligible subnational legislatures and special-status territories.

Earlier working notes incorrectly called this the “2025 index”. EIU's actual
2025 edition reports 26 full and 48 flawed democracies and moves France into the
full-democracy group. Refreshing the maintained targets requires a separate
country-by-country comparison; the existing 71-country audit must not be
presented as complete coverage of the newer edition.
[EIU's 2025 release](https://www.economistgroup.com/press-centre/economist-enterprise/eiu-democracy-index-2025-democracy-stabilises-after-eight-years-of-decline).

The tiers guide research priority. Catalogue inclusion depends on legislative
authority and a sufficiently verified official viewing service. Sources outside
these tiers can qualify. A country-level rating does not establish a regional
body's powers or broadcast quality, and documenting a source does not endorse
its government or democratic status.

## How complete is the inventory?

All 71 baseline countries have a recorded framework/directory reconciliation.
The [operational institution register](../data/discovery/institutions.json) brings
together the historical 1,122-entry regional ledger, 103 main national chambers,
named regional chambers and legislative organs, and the resolved scope decisions.
It retains exclusions, exact legal-instrument holds and predecessors. The older
652-body service register covers its original cohort; it is not the full current
institution inventory.

All 19 original scope questions have decisions: 14 qualify and five fall outside
the agreed boundary. The follow-up also records comparable regional autonomies,
territory-level findings and corrections to earlier classifications. Four US
unicameral chamber labels are aliases of existing institutions, rather than
additional bodies. Jurisdictions, chambers, shared formations and viewing services
are separate counts.

The [further 11-case legal review](../reports/discovery/2026-10-24-legal-holds-review.md)
closes the Guadeloupe, Guyane and Mayotte holds as **current default-scope
exclusions**, preserving their real regulatory/budget powers, Guadeloupe’s
historical legislation and explicit reopening tests. Eight instrument-specific
holds remain in Panama and Nagaland. The France finding is dated; generic
enabling procedures, a pending request, renamed assembly or national-government
ordinance do not establish a current local lawmaking grant.

This completes the recorded reconciliation pass, **not a guarantee that no
institution remains undiscovered**. Named legal holds remain unresolved, and
preferred names, current activity, service ownership and broadcasts require the
next validation phase. [Findings, country crosswalk and remaining holds](../reports/discovery/2026-10-24-inventory-reconciliation.md).

## Assess authority, not the name

For a new institution, retain an official constitutional, statutory or
institutional source establishing:

1. its position as a legislature in the relevant system of government;
2. its substantive law-making powers within its jurisdiction, and its budget
   and executive-scrutiny role;
3. its territorial and constitutional relationship, including any autonomy,
   free association or disputed status.

An elected membership, a televised meeting, parliamentary-style procedure,
budget approval or delegated local by-law powers alone are insufficient.
Ordinary city/town councils, intermunicipal planning councils, administrative
coordination bodies and service-delivery boards are outside the catalogue.

Names do not decide eligibility. A Swiss cantonal parliament or the Isle of
Man's Legislative Council can qualify despite the word “council”. Conversely,
an institution called an “assembly” is not automatically a parliament.
Territorial legislatures need not have sovereign or unlimited powers.

Autonomous regional legislation can be called an **ordinance** rather than an
Act. For a first-level or autonomous special-status assembly, a general power to
enact binding regional norms affecting rights or obligations, backed by budget and
executive-scrutiny powers, can satisfy this rule. Check the extent and legal basis of that power;
a narrow delegated service regulation alone remains insufficient. The ordinary
municipal exclusion still applies where municipalities have similar powers.

## Borderline and special cases

Keep a documented authority review before promoting regional assemblies whose
powers may resemble local government, including Nordic regional councils,
Japanese prefectural assemblies, Dutch provincial bodies and comparable
county/departmental assemblies. Do not infer a common answer from an English
translation of their names. Existing entries are reviewed individually rather
than silently removed.

The [22 October authority resolution](../reports/discovery/2026-10-22-bern-and-regional-authority.md)
confirms Nagasaki Prefectural Assembly and the twelve Dutch provincial councils
as eligible on this basis. Nagasaki's autonomous ordinances and Dutch provincial
ordinances are substantive regional law-making despite national-law limits.
This settles their authority gate; each broadcast service still needs its own
identity, access and source-conditions review. It does not decide eligibility
for Dutch Caribbean island councils or automatically publish other Japanese
prefectures. Skåne remains outside scope under its separately documented
regional service/local-government assessment.

Indigenous traditional congresses, special-territory councils and capital
district bodies need individual legal-authority assessment. An exception is
not established simply by geographic remoteness or special administrative
status. Appointed executives and administrations are not legislatures.

The regional treaty legislatures of Nunatsiavut, Nisg̱a’a, Tłı̨chǫ and the Cree
Nation of Eeyou Istchee, and India's ten Sixth Schedule territorial councils,
qualify on documented territorial lawmaking powers. Their local community and
village governments are distinct. Finland's, Norway's and Sweden's Sámi bodies
receive separate legal assessments; cultural autonomy or a consultation/consent
role alone does not establish an autonomous territorial legislature.

Free-associated states and constituent countries must retain their correct
relationships; they are not ordinary provinces of the audit's grouping
country. Disputed institutions require neutral status evidence. Existing
international institutional coverage is separately labelled supranational;
it does not create a general council-inclusion rule.

## Publication and other surfaces

Only an eligible institution with a validated official viewing service is
promoted. The published catalogue must offer a usable viewing route: direct
playback, an approved provider embed or an official viewing link. Unready feed
variants stay in `candidates/` with their evidence and review holds; they do not
belong in the viewing catalogue. The legislature inventory retains eligible
bodies even when no usable source has been published. Preserve the evidence for source ownership, chamber/committee
identity, access, source conditions and accessibility. An archive or audio
service may be useful when accurately described.

Every promotion must be reconciled across catalogue search, jurisdiction
relationships, the coverage map and schedule source coverage. A viewing page
does not establish stable HLS, and a meeting calendar does not establish an
implemented XMLTV parser. IPTV and EPG exports retain those separate gates.

Source rights also need separate findings for linking, embedding, independent
direct playback, rebroadcasting, recording/copying, other audiovisual reuse and
programme-data licensing. Retain dated source-specific terms, conditions and
uncertainties; public visibility alone establishes none of those permissions.

The broad research ledger retains excluded and unresolved bodies so future
research does not repeat the same searches. Its counts describe discovery
work, not the number of eligible parliaments or usable streams.

## Reviewed exclusions and priorities

The [23 October register](../reports/discovery/2026-10-23-legislative-priority.md) lists each excluded body with its specific scope rationale and primary sources. Historical media leads are retained outside the active validation queue. Reopen a scope decision on new authority evidence or an agreed scope change; a blocked player is a technical hold and a working player does not establish legislative authority.

The [24 October consistency review](../reports/discovery/2026-10-24-regional-scope-consistency.md)
supersedes 22 of those earlier exclusions: Croatia's 20 county assemblies and
Zagreb, plus Budapest, meet the regional ordinance test. Czech assemblies remain
eligible. The narrower Albanian, Hungarian county, Guyanese and Mongolian powers
retain specific exclusions. These current decisions are applied in the operational
register; the dated historical ledger remains available for provenance.

The [second authority pass](../reports/discovery/2026-10-23-authority-second-pass.md) resolves the remaining core state/cantonal/autonomous-community frameworks. Glarus and Appenzell Innerrhoden retain their Landsgemeinde distinctions; Indian union territories are not labelled states. Ordinary mainland French regional councils, Ceuta/Melilla and Christmas/Cocos shires are excluded under their separately documented powers. The later full pass assesses Corsica and French overseas arrangements individually.

The [full classification pass](../reports/discovery/2026-10-23-authority-completion.md)
assesses all 392 former generic reviews. A `specific_authority_evidence_hold`
records a concrete missing legal instrument, operative grant or allocation of
powers; it is neither eligibility clearance nor an exclusion. Each hold names
the evidence needed. `historical_institution_superseded` preserves a predecessor
and redirects current research to its successor. Neither belongs in the active
eligible-service queue.

The decisions distinguish Tobago's Bills requiring national enactment from
Rodrigues's own territorial regulations, and pending Corsican reform from
Martinique's enacted 2026 habilitation. Indigenous customary territorial rules
can qualify when their actual normative and financial/executive-control powers
are established. Consultation, collective land ownership and cultural
representation alone do not establish that authority.

Match coverage by country and institutional/source identity. A shared region
name is insufficient: Córdoba in Colombia is distinct from Córdoba in Argentina,
just as Florida in Uruguay is distinct from the US state.
